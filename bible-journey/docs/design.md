# Phraser game design

## From the book to the first puzzle

The book opens onto the illustrated title and menu. Starting a new game takes three choices: age, play mode and companion. The four age groups are 4–7, 8–12, 13–17 and adults. Solo play and two-player pass-and-play use separate saves. Continue returns a player to their saved stage. The illustrated “Every story opens a new path” folio becomes available from character selection after four landmarks.

## What the player sees

Seven maps each contain four ordinary landmarks. A single game screen alternates between automatic travel, a picture, answer feedback and the next picture. Ordinary questions draw on shared Bible scenes. Companion choice determines which landscape, duck and treasure questions appear. Each landmark has five illustrated questions. At the final landmark each picture invites a comparison with another passage.

The map draws four waypoints over its walkable path. Travel lasts about 3.5 seconds of active time, with the character moving automatically, evolving at milestones and returning to the lower right of the question screen. A short passage summary appears in the sky between landmarks. Reduced motion and pause are available. One or two treasures appear during travel per map. They remain hidden until their discovery animation. The treasure guide shows item types and their uses.

The question artwork, clue buttons, answer choices, wallet and hearts occupy the same screen. A correct answer shows an explanation and automatically advances. One animated coin travels to the wallet when a token reward is earned. The clue rail is beside the opening picture. An additional image costs one token and a written hint costs two. Letter help and treasure stock are shown where available. Only the current slide is shown.

Ordinary mistakes cost one heart. Out of hearts, the player may retry the current landmark. Earlier landmarks, purchases already consumed and paid rewards remain recorded. Timed treasure attempts end after one choice or 45 seconds. A clay jar gives a token, the staff a heart, the lamp a written hint and the seal a letter. The timer pauses with an overlay, loss of focus or unavailable artwork. Two-player mode rotates turns and tracks each player’s score against a shared supply of hearts. 

## Visual language

Worn dark brown, terracotta, warm cream and muted gold connect the title, selection screens and quiz. The buttons use inset outlines and textured carved faces. Hebrew uses bundled Frank Ruhl Libre and English uses EB Garamond. Pixel-art companions coexist with illustrated picture riddles in age-specific treatments. The youngest players get storybook drawings with simpler shapes.

The manuscript lettering and ink illustrations in [Pentiment](https://www.obsidian.net/games/pentiment) and the illustrated travel map in [Sorcery!](https://www.inklestudios.com/2013/03/12/sorcery-screenshots) informed the visual comparison. Existing duck artwork, names and Hebrew typography anchor the game in its own style.
